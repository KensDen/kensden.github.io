Claude Certified Associate: Foundations (CCAO-F) Study Guide

A plan for the Associate exam, in priority order, with a practice drill to go with it.

Unofficial and not affiliated with Anthropic. Written from Anthropic's exam guide for CCAO-F and Anthropic's public documentation, checked 30 September 2026. Exam details change: confirm everything below against the current exam guide before you book.

Practice with the companion drill: Claude Cert Drill.

Exam facts

Exam codeCCAO-F
Items60
FormatMultiple choice and multiple response (each item says how many to pick)
Time120 minutes, about 2 minutes per item
Passing score720 on a scaled range of 100 to 1,000
DeliveryPearson VUE, online proctored or at a test center
Fee$99 list
Validity12 months

Two things to know up front.

The credential lasts 12 months, not the two or three years many certifications give. Renewal on time is a free, unproctored assessment covering what changed. Let it lapse and you sit the full exam again. Put the renewal date on your calendar the day you pass.

Multiple-response items are where prepared candidates lose points. Each one says how many answers to pick. Read that number every time.

The blueprint, and where the risk usually is

#DomainWeight
1Prompting and Task Execution14%
2Output Evaluation and Validation21%
3Product and Model Selection12%
4Workflow Integration and Solution Design16%
5Configuration and Knowledge Management12%
6Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use15%
7Troubleshooting and Optimization10%

Calibrate first. This is not a security exam and not a developer exam. The candidate it is written for uses Claude every day in operations, marketing, project management or a similar role. Good judgment about work carries you a long way.

Where experienced people slip. Daily use does not guarantee you know the whole feature surface. Domains 3 and 5, 24% together, test exactly that: model tier tradeoffs, context and memory, Projects configuration, connectors and instructions. Most candidates' study hours belong there.

Study plan, in priority order

First: Domains 3 and 5 (24%, the feature surface)

Domain 3, Product and Model Selection. Choosing among Claude's features (Projects, research, chat, artifacts), telling the model tiers apart, matching a model to cost, speed and quality needs, and managing context and memory: when to restart, summarize or carry forward.

  • Read the current model overview in Anthropic's documentation. Lineups change, so read it now rather than trusting what you remember.
  • For each tier, be able to say in one sentence what kind of task it fits. High-volume, low-complexity drafting belongs on a faster, cheaper tier, not the most capable one.
  • Get precise on context limits and memory, and on when to start a fresh conversation versus summarize and carry forward. That judgment shows up as scenario items.

Domain 5, Configuration and Knowledge Management. Setting up Projects with instructions and knowledge, managing uploaded knowledge and connectors, writing effective instructions, and keeping configurations current.

  • Go past your personal habits into the full feature surface: what a Project accepts as knowledge, how connectors attach, and how project instructions differ from steering inside one conversation.
  • Build one throwaway Project from scratch with instructions, an uploaded file and a connector, just to touch every setting once.
  • Don't skim the maintenance objective. Knowing when instructions and knowledge go stale is its own testable idea.

Primary sources: Claude Help Center for app features, and Claude Docs for models and the platform. Review Projects, Artifacts, Memory, Skills and Code Execution in particular.

Second: Domain 2 (21%, the largest domain)

Judging outputs for accuracy and completeness, spotting hallucinations, inconsistency and bias, fact-checking, deciding when a human must review, adapting output for an audience, and choosing the output format.

  • Learn hallucination patterns, especially fabricated specifics that look authoritative, like citation numbers or exact figures.
  • Treat format choice (an artifact, inline text or structured data) as a judgment call. It is a product-feature question dressed as an evaluation question, and it is easy to under-study.

Third: Domains 1, 4 and 7 (40%, confirm rather than learn)

Prompting, workflow integration and stakeholder communication, and troubleshooting prompts that underperform. Read each objective and rate yourself honestly. Anything unfamiliar becomes a study item; the rest you likely do already.

Last: Domain 6 (15%, verify)

Appropriate and inappropriate uses, data sensitivity and privacy, organizational AI policy and ethics. If you work under an AI use policy already, read the objectives, confirm nothing surprises you and spend the time elsewhere.

Resources, in order of authority

  1. The official exam guide for CCAO-F. Its blueprint section is the checklist: rate yourself against every objective. Its sample items show the question style.
  2. Anthropic's prep course for CCAO-F. The primary prep material where you have access to it.
  3. Anthropic's documentation: the Claude Help Center and Claude Docs.
  4. People who have sat it. They can describe difficulty and emphasis, but not content.
  5. Third-party practice sets, including the Claude Cert Drill. Treat all of them, mine included, as unofficial and never as a substitute for items 1 to 3.

Before exam day

  • Seat booked at Pearson VUE
  • Fee confirmed at checkout
  • Government photo ID, unexpired, with your name exactly as registered
  • Testing online: desk clear of notes, phone and second monitor
  • Rated yourself against every objective in all seven domains
  • Built one throwaway Project with instructions, knowledge and a connector
  • Read the current model overview within the last week
  • One "Timed mock" run on the drill, scored
  • Renewal date on the calendar, 12 months from the day you pass
